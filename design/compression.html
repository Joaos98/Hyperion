Timeline compression

A career is twelve to forty years long and almost entirely uneventful. Mapping time to pixels honestly produces a very tall, very empty column where the interesting part is a thin band at the top. The rule is not a ruler — it carries continuity and state, while exact duration is carried in text where it can be read precisely.

Two thirds of the column is empty, the last eighteen months hold half the content, and a two-month contract would be four pixels tall — too short to hold its own name.

The rule

  1. Stretches that hold events render to scale, with a floor: no position shorter than one line of type, whatever its real duration.
  2. Stretches with no events, longer than three months, fold into a single marked row that states exactly what it hid — 2y 4m · nothing logged. One click opens it.
  3. Nothing is ever capped or squashed silently. Time is either shown to scale or explicitly folded and labelled, the same rule the app already applies to Converted amounts.
  4. A fold never breaks the rule. Inside a position it stays thick — you were still employed through the quiet years. Between positions it stays hairline. Continuity is the whole idea of the Pillar and compression must not cost it.
  5. Year markers survive folds, so orientation never depends on what is currently open.

This is the diff-fold idiom — 47 unchanged lines — which is honest, expandable, reversible, and already fluent to anyone who reads code. It needs no continuous distortion, no fisheye maths, and no zoom mode.

What this decides for the Timeline view

Three consequences worth carrying into the wireframes, because they close questions the plan left open:

  1. Vertical, and no zoom control. Folding replaces zoom entirely — there are no levels, no modes, and nothing to learn. Scrolling is the only navigation.
  2. No minimap. A second proportional strip would restore at-a-glance duration, but duration is already stated exactly in every badge, and text beats a graphic for a figure you want precisely. One rule, not two.
  3. Density is a preference, not a fact. The three-month fold threshold is the one number here worth exposing in settings — some people log weekly, some twice a year, and the right threshold differs by an order of magnitude between them.

And the day-one case still holds: two months of data produces no folds at all, a short rule, and one position. The mechanism only appears once there is something to hide, which is the property a compression scheme most needs and most often lacks.
